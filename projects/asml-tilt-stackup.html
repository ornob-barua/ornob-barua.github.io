ASML Tilt Tolerance Alignment Stack-Up Analysis

Built a worst-case tilt tolerance stack-up to find why a supplier-built optical assembly kept failing its alignment specification.

Problem

An optical assembly transitioned to a new supplier consistently failed optical alignment specifications despite the supplier receiving the same engineering drawings and assembly documentation as the incumbent supplier. The source of the misalignment was unclear.

Method

  • Identified critical components and geometric tolerances contributing to optical misalignment using GD&T and engineering drawings.
  • Converted component-level linear and positional tolerances into equivalent angular misalignment to develop a worst-case tilt tolerance stack-up.
  • Developed an automated Excel tool to calculate predicted relative tilt between the optical assembly's alignment plates and compare results against optical alignment test data.
  • Applied the same analysis to a comparable optical assembly manufactured in-house to identify differences in tolerance allocation and drawing requirements.

Result / Next Steps

  • Identified significant differences in GD&T requirements and tolerance allocation between the in-house and supplier-manufactured assemblies.
  • Determined that the supplier's existing drawing tolerances could produce worst-case misalignment exceeding the allowable optical alignment specification.
  • Established a path toward improving alignment performance through drawing revisions, component-level GD&T review, and evaluation of the supplier's manufacturing and assembly processes.
