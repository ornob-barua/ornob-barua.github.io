MEMS & Cleanroom Manufacturing

Microfluidic Channel Fabrication

Fabricated SiO₂, glass, and PDMS microfluidic channels using cleanroom microfabrication processes and experimentally characterized channel performance.

Problem

Fabricate SiO₂, glass, and PDMS microchannels in the UCSB teaching cleanroom and test whether they support electroosmotic flow.

Method

  • Used thermal oxidation, contact photolithography, BHF and KOH wet etching, PDMS molding, and bonding.
  • Checked thickness, depth, width, and sidewall angle with reflectometry, profilometry, and microscopy.
  • Tested bonded devices with current monitoring, using KCl solutions and platinum electrodes.

Result / Next Steps

  • Reached near-target depths: about 4 µm (SiO₂), 2 µm (glass), and 3 µm (PDMS molds).
  • Widths matched the mask, and KOH sidewalls were close to the theoretical 54.7°.
  • Most devices showed little to no flow. One glass channel behaved like electroosmotic flow, and one SiO₂ channel reached µA currents that may have been leakage.
  • Bonding was one of the biggest causes of lost yield.

What I'd Do Differently Next Time

  • Make the channels deeper and wider.
  • Be more careful with PDMS bonding, and use reversible PDMS bonding for all samples.
  • Complete the processing in a cleaner environment.
  • Use material properties to my advantage, and avoid etch calibrations where the bottom material acts as an etch stop.
