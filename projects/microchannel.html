MEMS & Cleanroom Manufacturing

Microfluidic Channel Fabrication

Fabricated SiO₂, glass, and PDMS microfluidic channels using cleanroom microfabrication processes and experimentally characterized channel performance.

Problem

Microfluidic devices are highly sensitive to fabrication and bonding quality, and small defects can block or alter flow. The goal was to fabricate SiO₂, glass, and PDMS microchannels in the UCSB teaching cleanroom and test whether each could support electroosmotic flow.

Method

  • Performed thermal oxidation, contact photolithography on a Karl Süss MJB3 aligner, BHF and KOH wet etching, PDMS molding, and device bonding.
  • Calibrated etch times to reach target depths of 4 µm for SiO₂ channels, 2 µm for glass channels, and 3 µm for PDMS molds.
  • Verified oxide thickness, channel depth, channel width, and sidewall angle using reflectometry, profilometry, and optical microscopy.
  • Tested bonded devices with current monitoring, using KCl electrolyte solutions and platinum electrodes placed in the channel reservoirs.

Results

  • Produced channels close to design targets: SiO₂ depths near 4 µm, glass depths of about 2 µm, and PDMS mold heights near 3 µm.
  • Channel widths closely matched the mask dimensions, and KOH sidewall angles were close to the theoretical 54.7°.
  • Current monitoring gave mixed results: most devices showed only pA to nA currents, indicating little to no flow.
  • One glass channel showed trends close to the expected electroosmotic response. One SiO₂ channel reached µA-level currents, though leakage through the substrate or oxide could not be ruled out.
  • Identified bonding as one of the largest contributors to reduced yield, and recommended deeper channels and improved bonding for future devices.
