ME 108 Project 2

C-Clamp FEA & Topology Optimization

Reduced the mass of a 6-inch ductile iron C-clamp by 12.7% while increasing its factor of safety from 1.282 to 1.329.

Problem

C-clamps are often heavy, with a very robust body. Removing mass can improve ergonomics and reduce material cost, but the clamp still has to carry its rated load. Working with Camden Smith, the goal was to minimize the mass of a 6-inch C-clamp body while maintaining at least its original factor of safety.

Method

  • Used a commercially available ductile cast iron clamp (McMaster-Carr 5033A5) as the baseline, removing the screw and handle to focus on the structural body.
  • Simplified the threaded region to a cylindrical cut and applied the 2,400 lb (10,675 N) rated load, using loading and fixed constraints based on established C-clamp FEA methods.
  • Ran a static study in SolidWorks to establish a baseline factor of safety of 1.282.
  • Ran two iterations of topology optimization to identify where material could be removed, then redesigned the body and re-ran the static study.

Results

  • Reduced body mass from 2.44 lb to 2.13 lb, a 12.7% reduction.
  • Increased the factor of safety from 1.282 to 1.329, satisfying the constraint.
  • Reduced the mass of the middle grip section by 25.8%, from 0.93 lb to 0.69 lb.
  • These are simulation results. Proposed next steps are an FEA study of hand grip pressure on the handle and physical load testing of a machined clamp.
