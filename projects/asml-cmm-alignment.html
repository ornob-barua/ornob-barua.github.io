CMM-Based Optical Alignment Characterization

Used a portable CMM and MATLAB to turn raw measurements into optical alignment metrics and isolate sources of tilt.

Problem

Direct optical alignment measurements could not be reliably performed on-site for the supplier-manufactured assembly. A portable coordinate measuring machine (CMM) was therefore used to characterize the assembly's geometry and investigate potential sources of optical misalignment.

Method

  • Used a portable CMM to measure critical assembly features, including planes and circular geometries, and establish a coordinate reference system.
  • Extracted and processed raw CMM measurements in MATLAB to reconstruct component geometry and calculate angular relationships between selected surfaces.
  • Developed custom calculations to resolve relative tilt into independent X- and Y-axis components, providing more meaningful alignment measurements than the native CMM software.
  • Introduced controlled angular offsets to the assembly and evaluated their effects on measured tilt to validate the analysis.
  • Constructed a theoretical optical axis from measured geometric features to establish a reference for comparison with the optical alignment specification.

Result / Next Steps

  • Identified the X-axis as the dominant contributor to measured assembly tilt, narrowing the investigation toward components and tolerances along that direction.
  • Determined that a gap smaller than the diameter of a human hair between assembly components could introduce substantial angular misalignment, highlighting a critical assembly interface.
  • Developed a MATLAB-based workflow to translate raw CMM measurements into physically meaningful optical alignment metrics and isolate potential sources of accumulated tilt.
  • Next steps include correlating the reconstructed optical axis with the actual optical alignment specification and using the results to guide component GD&T revisions and assembly process improvements.
